## The job, in one sentence

Every round, the platform sends my agent signed tasks. Each one describes a payment condition and a proof, and gives me **60 seconds** to answer APPROVE or REJECT — with a Groth16 proof the platform re-verifies against its own key. Get it right: +12. Approve a bad one: −6. Reject a good one: −3. Time out: −2. Answer without a valid proof: −2.

The twist: the proof does not just say "I checked". It says "I checked *this exact condition*, with *this exact key*, for *this exact recipient and amount*, before *this exact expiry* — and here is the cryptography to prove it." A proof that verifies perfectly can still authorize the wrong action. That is the whole game.

## The 60 seconds, step by step

My agent is **AKANG-GK** — a Node.js stdlib HTTP server on Railway (`web-production-912eebcf.up.railway.app`), code public at `github.com/haniefff/agent-games-season-01` (`gatekeeper/`). A task arrives as a signed JSON envelope at `POST /agent`. Here is what happens inside, in order:

**1. Verify the signature before parsing anything.** The server checks the HMAC-SHA256 signature over the raw request body first. A bad or missing signature gets a 401 and nothing else — the envelope is never even parsed. This is the same instinct as my ClaimGuard gate: never let an unauthenticated input reach the decision logic.

**2. Check the seven traps, in a fixed order.** The envelope carries a condition (minimum balance, public balance, expiry block, current block, recipient, amount, transfer cap) and a proof with its verification key. The checks:

| Trap | What it means | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| VALID_CONDITION (~50% of tasks) | Everything checks out | APPROVE |
| VK_SUBSTITUTION | The proof used a different key than the condition registered | REJECT |
| THRESHOLD_MISMATCH | Public balance below the required minimum | REJECT |
| PROOF_REUSED | This proof was already used for an earlier task | REJECT, no new proof |
| EXPIRED_CONDITION | Current block past the expiry block | REJECT |
| RECIPIENT_MISMATCH | The money would go somewhere the condition did not name | REJECT |
| AMOUNT_OVERFLOW | Amount above the single-transfer cap | REJECT |

**3. Prove the decision — or admit you cannot.** The decision goes into the official circuit (`gatekeeper.wasm`, 1,755,820 bytes; `gatekeeper_final.zkey`, 309,750 bytes; Groth16/BN128, 13 public signals, verification-key hash `0x6b79c6…d77b0`). The circuit enforces one rule above all: **the decision must equal the computed verdict.** Approve if and only if every check passes. I verified this the hard way in local tests: a valid condition with decision=0 is *refused by the circuit itself* — a wrong decision is unprovable, not merely rejected by the platform. The proof binds the decision to the task (`taskId = keccak256(task_id) mod the field prime`, confirmed live when the practice task passed) and to a salt that never appears in the public signals.

**4. Fail closed on the expensive errors.** Two cases get special handling. If a task is a resend of one already answered, it is PROOF_REUSED: REJECT with no proof, because re-proving the same task is exactly what the trap is for. If proof generation itself fails, the server still returns the *correct* decision without a proof — that costs −2, while flipping the decision to something provable would cost −6 or −3. A missing proof is cheaper than a wrong answer. Malformed tasks get REJECT with reason `INVALID_TASK` and no proof at all.

## What the circuit cannot do for you

The circuit proves the decision follows from the inputs. It does not prove the inputs are the ones you think they are — that is exactly what the seven traps are for, and why the platform re-verifies against *its own* registered key rather than trusting the one in the envelope. Verification of the proof and verification of *what was proven* are two different jobs, and the agent has to do both in under 60 seconds, every time, for 8 tasks a round across 8 daily rounds.

The other thing the circuit cannot do: make a bad policy good. A proof that your agent followed its policy is powerful. A proof that the policy itself was sound is a different question — and the one my Steal the VFY article is about.

## What this is not

- **Not a score report.** Points are not confirmed. The 12 proofs are a health fact, not a standings claim.
- **Not a guarantee.** A practice task and a healthy endpoint do not promise a single scored point. Rounds 4–8 run 17:30–03:30 WIB through 20 Nov; anything can time out, any proof can fail to generate, any trap can be misread.
- **Not finished.** The platform re-verifies proofs itself; no separate zkVerify submission is made per task, and if the platform ever requires one, that is a separate integration.
